# Chess Opening Trainer — Spec

## Overview

A browser-based chess practice program. It pulls the user's studies from lichess.org and quizzes the user on the correct next moves.

## Lichess access

- Covers all of the user's studies, including private ones.
- Authenticates with a personal API token that the user generates once in Lichess account settings (Preferences → API access tokens).
- The token needs the `study:read` scope so private studies can be read.
- The user enters the token once; the browser remembers it (localStorage) so it isn't asked for again.
- The app should offer a way to clear or replace the stored token (on the Options page).
- A new token is checked with Lichess before it is saved; if Lichess rejects it, the app says so and doesn't store it.

### Fetching studies

- Studies are pulled from Lichess only when the user asks, via an "Update studies" button. The first visit (nothing cached yet) fetches automatically once the token is entered.
- Fetched studies are cached in the browser between visits. Use IndexedDB rather than localStorage, since a full export of many studies can exceed localStorage's ~5 MB limit.

## Selecting what to practice

The user chooses the practice set at any of these levels:

- **Individual chapters:** one or more specific chapters, possibly from different studies.
- **A whole study:** all chapters in one study.
- **A set of studies:** all chapters in several chosen studies.
- **Everything:** all chapters in all of the user's studies.

**Layout:** a checkbox list of the user's studies, each expandable to show its chapters with their own checkboxes.

- Studies are listed alphabetically; chapters keep their order within the study.
- **Opening name:** hovering over a chapter's name on the Studies page shows the opening from the chapter's Lichess PGN tags, e.g. "Italian Game: Classical Variation, Greco Gambit, Traditional Line (C54)": the `Opening` tag, then the `ECO` code in brackets. If only one of the tags is there, just that is shown; with neither, there's no hover text. It's shown in the app's own small pop-up (like the comment bubbles), which appears after about 0.7 seconds (the user's decision in the UI clean-up, so it doesn't cover the moves that open when the name is clicked; before that all the app's pop-ups appeared immediately, and the delay was 0.15 seconds, then 1 second, before that), instead of the browser's standard tooltip, whose delay can't be set. (The tag describes the chapter as a whole, from its main line; variations deeper in the chapter may be other named openings.)
- Each study row shows its name, then in grey its number of chapters, e.g. "Ruy Lopez · 2 chapters".
- Each chapter row shows its name and side circle, then in grey its **number of lines**, e.g. "Main line Chigorin ● · 5 lines" ("1 line" when there's one; a chapter with no testable lines shows no count). (The alternative moves used to follow, e.g. "· 2 alternative moves", with an explanation on hover; the user removed them, 6 October 2026. History: the counts were first on the study rows, as "4 chapters · 37 lines (+5 side variations)", then "· 5 alternative moves"; the user moved the line and alternative-move counts to the chapter rows.)
- Each study row's **chapter count** (e.g. "· 7 chapters") underlines on hover and shows or hides the study's chapters when clicked, the same as the study's name (the user's request, 6 October 2026).
- An **alternative move** (called a "side variation" until the user asked for clearer wording) is another move for the user in the study, at one of the user's turns on a practiced line (e.g. 3. Bb5 where the main move is 3. Bc4). Each counts once, however many moves follow it; alternative moves inside other alternative moves aren't counted. They aren't practiced as lines (see Branches) and aren't included in the line count. The counts appear on chapter rows; study rows show only the number of chapters. The line count doesn't change with "Main line only"; the count at the top of the right-hand panel shows how many selected lines will actually be practiced.

- Ticking a study ticks all its chapters; unticking it unticks them all.
- If only some of a study's chapters are ticked, the study's checkbox shows a partial (indeterminate) state.
- Chapter rows are indented one tab (32px) to the right of their study's checkbox, on all screen sizes, so chapters clearly sit under their study. A chapter's two triangles sit under its study's ▸ and checkbox, so the chapter's checkbox stays one tab in.
- **A chapter's move tree:** each chapter row has its own ▸ button (like a study's) that opens the chapter's moves underneath it, indented one more step; ▾ closes them (hover: "Show moves" / "Hide moves"). Only the ▸ opens them; clicking the chapter's name still ticks or unticks it. A chapter with no testable lines (e.g. an unsupported variant) has no ▸ button, just a space.
  - The moves are shown as a tree, the way Lichess shows a study: the **main line** always shows, flowing along in Lichess style ("1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 Nc6 3. Bc4"). After a move that has alternatives, a small **+** button opens those variations underneath, each on its own indented row (with a thin line down the left), each in the same style and with its own + buttons; **−** closes them. The main line then continues on a new row ("3… Bc5 4. c3"). Variations start closed. Every move and variation in the study is included.
  - **All-variations triangle:** each chapter row has a second small triangle, just right of the moves triangle and left of the checkbox (so there are two side by side). It points right (▸) when the variations are closed and down (▾) when they're all open. ▸ opens every variation in the chapter at once, including variations inalternative moves and alternative moves, and also opens the chapter's moves if they're closed; ▾ closes all the variations (the moves stay shown). The individual + / − buttons keep working afterwards, and they don't change the triangle: after ▸, it stays pointing down even when single variations are closed with their −, until the triangle itself is clicked (which closes all of them). Likewise, opening every variation one by one doesn't turn it down. (At first it pointed down only while every variation was open; the user asked for it to change only when clicked.) Hover: "Show all variations" / "Hide all variations". A chapter without variations has an empty space there, so checkboxes stay lined up. (This replaced a "+ all / − all" button above the main line, at the user's request.)
  - All moves are in the standard text color (see Progress tracking for the highlighting of weak moves, and Comments and glyphs for glyph colors); move numbers are grey; **alternative moves at the user's moves** (never practiced), and everything after them, are in italics with no score. Move glyphs and comment bubbles appear as in the practice move list, following the annotations icon. Comment bubbles are the same soft blue as everywhere.
  - Every move has the small hover board (see Colored moves and the hover board). A side-variation move shows the position after it, like an opponent's move, labeled "Alternative move, not tested", with the move drawn as a white arrow, or an amber arrow if the study marks it ?, ?? or ?!.
  - Open chapters and open variations stay open while the user moves around the app (like open studies), until the page is closed or reloaded.
  - History: this replaced a list of the chapter's lines (in study order, each with its colored moves, weakest score, evaluation and a "main line" label, with "Show all N lines" for chapters over 100 lines), at the user's request, together with the switch to per-position scores.
- An "All" checkbox at the top selects or clears everything.
- Each chapter and each study shows the user's average move accuracy next to it, shown as just the percentage, e.g. "82%", with a one-sentence explanation in the hover pop-up (the same immediate pop-up as the opening names): "Average move accuracy over the last 10 attempts" (first "Accuracy over the last 10 attempts", the user's wording after grading moved to points; "Average move" was added with the move/line accuracy terms). (It said "82% right", then "82% correct moves", until the user asked for just the number with a hover explanation; the pop-up then said "How often you play your moves here correctly: the average over the 5 moves you've tried, each over its last 10 tries.") The figure is the average score of the moves the user has tried across all its lines (see Progress tracking), highlighted with the same ranges as the moves (see Progress tracking). Moves not tried yet don't count toward the average; if nothing has been tried, nothing is shown. A chapter with no testable lines (e.g. an unsupported variant, or no moves for the user) still says "no lines to test". (It used to show a miss rate per line.)

**End of each line in the move tree:** the **last move of each line** you practice is **underlined** in a chapter's move tree, so each line's end is easy to see (the user's request). *Claude's choice, accepted by the user:* the ends of untested alternative moves' variations (in italics) aren't underlined, since they aren't lines you practice.

**Clicking names:** clicking a study's name shows or hides its chapters (the same as its ▸), and clicking a chapter's name shows or hides its moves as a tree, starting with the main line (the same as its ▸); Enter or Space on a focused name does the same. Clicking a chapter's **number of lines** (e.g. "3 lines") shows or hides **all its variations** (showing them also shows the moves, and hiding them leaves the main line showing). Each chapter has **one ▸**, for its moves (the user's decision in the UI clean-up; a second ▸ used to open all its variations, the same as the number of lines); a chapter without variations has a plain count. (The user's request.) Selecting or deselecting a study or chapter is done only with its checkbox (clicking a name used to tick its checkbox; the user changed this). *Claude's choices, accepted by the user:* a name underlines on hover to show it can be clicked; a chapter with no lines to test (e.g. an unsupported variant) has a plain name; the chapter name's opening pop-up stays.

**Layout:** the list of studies and chapters is on the left, with the "All" checkbox at the top of the list, lined up with the studies' checkboxes. The selection controls are in a panel on the right: the count of chapters and lines, e.g. "2 chapters selected" (without the number of lines, the user's request; it used to add e.g. "· 4 lines"). (It also held a "Start practice" button and the "Opponent's moves" and "Where lines start" radio buttons; the user removed them: the Studies page only selects studies and chapters, and practice starts from the Lines and Positions pages.) (The "Show annotations" and "Show chapter names" checkboxes used to follow; they were replaced by icons on the practice page, which were later removed too.) Longer descriptions of these options show as hover tooltips. The panel stays in view while the list scrolls. On narrower screens (under about 800px) the panel moves above the list.

**Hidden studies:** a study the user no longer wants to see can be hidden; individual chapters can't (the user's choice).
- **The top eye:** a small eye icon in the presets bar, just right of the presets' pencil (the user moved it there as a more natural place; it was at the right-hand end of the "All" row). With a line through it (the normal state), hidden studies aren't in the list and study rows have no eye. Without the line, hidden studies appear in the list in grey (dimmed) and every study row gets its own eye. Hover: "Show hidden studies" / "Stop showing hidden studies". It's always clickable, even when nothing is hidden (it's also the way to hide studies), and isn't remembered: the list always opens with the line through it. There's no count of hidden studies.

It won't be practiced."), an eye with a line through it for a hidden one (click to unhide it). So hiding a study is: open the top eye, click the study's eye, close the top eye.
- If every study is hidden (with the top eye closed), the list says "All your studies are hidden. Click the eye at the top to see them."
- **Excluded from practice:** hiding a study unticks all its chapters, and while it's hidden its checkboxes are disabled, so it's never practiced. "All" ticks only visible studies. Choosing a saved selection skips chapters in hidden studies and says how many it skipped. Practice itself also ignores any chapter in a hidden study.
- Its stats are kept, so unhiding brings everything back as it was (its chapters stay unticked until ticked again). The automatic Lichess downloads still fetch data for hidden studies.
- Hidden studies are remembered between visits, included in backups, and survive Update studies (a study deleted on Lichess is dropped from the hidden list).
- History: first built with a "Show hidden (2)" text button and an eye that appeared at the end of a study row on hover; the user changed it to the top eye and per-study eyes after the name.

**Saved selections** (the user's request; they replaced a single set of favorites): any number of named chapter selections, on the Studies page only (the user's decision):
- **Called "Presets" on screen** (the user's request): the drop-down's label is "**Presets:**", and the buttons' tooltips, messages and Help say "preset" (e.g. "Delete the preset "Sicilian lines"?"). *Claude's choice, accepted by the user:* all the on-screen wording says "preset", not just the label; the code and stored data keep the name "saved selections".

- A **drop-down** lists them by name, and only them: no "Choose a saved selection" entry at the top of the list (the user's request). *Claude's choice, accepted by the user:* when none is chosen (before the first choice, or after Delete), the closed drop-down shows "None chosen" (or "No presets yet"), **in grey** (the user's request), which isn't in the open list. Choosing one ticks exactly its chapters, replacing the current ticks and leaving each study open or closed as it was. Chapters no longer on Lichess, or in hidden studies, are skipped. **No message** is shown (the user's request; a fading message used to say e.g. "Sicilian lines: 12 chapters selected.", with how many chapters were skipped). The chosen selection is remembered between visits.

While the ticks differ from the chosen preset, its name is **greyed out** in the closed drop-down (the user's request; it first said e.g. "Favorites (changed)"). ### Chapters that can't be tested

- Chapters in a Lichess variant other than standard chess (e.g. Crazyhouse) show "variant not supported" and contribute no lines.
- Each chapter is capped at 5,000 lines, so a very large study can't freeze the page.

### Describe what to practice (removed)

- There used to be an optional "Describe what to practice" box on the Studies page: the user typed a request such as "all openings where I am white", and Claude (Haiku 4.5, via Anthropic's JavaScript SDK in the browser, with the user's own Anthropic API key) ticked the matching chapters. It was removed at the user's request (it was upkeep that other changes kept touching, and the app no longer stores an Anthropic key). Everything needed to rebuild it, including its full spec, the instructions sent to Claude and the code, is in `archive/natural-language-selection.md`, which the app never loads.
- On first opening after the change, the app deletes the stored Anthropic key and the natural-language setting from the browser. Older backups' natural-language setting is ignored.

### Opponent's moves and Where lines start (removed)

- The Studies page used to have a **Start practice** button and two sets of radio buttons: **Opponent's moves** (Main line only, Prefer most common moves, Prefer opponent's mistakes, Prefer lines I miss, All moves equally), which weighted which line came next, and **Where lines start** (From the start, Partway through, Test single positions). The Practice page's idle screen also had a Start practice button. The user removed all of them: the Studies page is only for selecting studies and chapters, and practice starts from the **Lines** and **Positions** pages (later moved to the Practice page's chooser). Lines are always played from move 1; single positions are tested from the Positions page.
- Their stored settings are ignored (and dropped on the next save), and older backups that contain them restore fine.

### Depth limit (removed)

- Lines are always practiced to their end. There used to be an optional per-chapter depth limit ("to move _x_", a number box next to each chapter, ending lines after the user's last move at or before move _x_). It was removed at the user's request and replaced by the **End line here** button, later replaced by **Skip** (see Skip).
- **Stats carried over:** when the app first opens after the change (and when restoring an older backup that has depth limits), each shortened line's move results are copied to every full line that continues it; the moves up to the old limit are the same, so each move keeps its record. Where a full line already had results for a move, the copied ones are added after them, keeping the last 10. The shortened lines' own stats are then removed (unless the shortened line is itself a full line), and the stored limits are forgotten. Backups no longer include depth limits.

## Quiz

### Side to play

- The user plays the side the chapter's board is oriented to on Lichess (white or black); the app plays the other side's moves.
- The board is shown from the user's side.
- The practice panel doesn't say "You play white/black"; the board's orientation already shows it. (The Studies page shows the side with a small white or black circle immediately to the right of each chapter's name, with no wording; hovering over it shows "You play white" or "You play black". It used to say "you play white/black" with the circle in its own column. A chapter that can't be tested shows its reason, e.g. "variant not supported", instead of a circle. The Help page describes it as "You play the side at the bottom of the board".)
- Orientation comes from the `Orientation` PGN tag, so study exports must be requested with `orientation=true`. If the tag is missing, default to white.

### Branches

- **Opponent's move:** the opponent plays the move of the line being practised; which line comes up is decided by the Practice page's chooser (see Choosing what to practise).
- **User's move:** only the main-line move counts as correct, and lines always follow the main move at the user's turns (alternative moves at the user's move are never practiced as lines). When the user plays a move that the study has as an alternative move at that point:
  - **Marked ?, ?? or ?!:** the move is taken back straight away, like any other error (the user's decision, 6 October 2026; it used to stay on the board with the opponent's reply for about 3 seconds). The status's first line says what it is, e.g. "Alternative move marked as a blunder", then, if the study gives the opponent's reply in that variation, **"Your study answers 2… ♘c6"** (the user chose this wording from Claude's suggestions), then the masters and Lichess lines (see Wrong moves), **all in red**; it fades to "Your move (…)" after 3 seconds like other errors. It counts as a wrong try, and the move is recorded as one of the user's wrong moves. Its study comment still appears only with Hint. (It used to say "Qh5?? is one of your alternative moves, marked as a blunder. The opponent replies 2… Nc6.", and before that "Qh5?? is marked as a blunder in your study.")
  - **Not marked:** the status's first line is **"Alternative move, not your preferred move"**, not red (the user's wording, 6 October 2026; it used to be "Bc4 is one of your alternative moves"), followed by the masters and Lichess lines (see Wrong moves), each coloured by its own rule. The move is taken back straight away. It isn't a wrong try, but it costs 2 points (see Grading an attempt) and is recorded in the attempt; the line then counts as not clean (not "No errors."). The message doesn't name the main move, so it doesn't give the answer away.
  - Any other wrong move gets a message in the same style (see Unlimited tries).
  - The Help page explains this.

### Making moves

- When the user moves a pawn to the last rank, a small picker asks which piece to promote to (queen, rook, bishop or knight).

### Comments and glyphs

(There used to be an annotations icon, a speech bubble in the practice panel's icon group, that hid them while a line was being played; the user removed it so they always show. Its stored setting and its entry in backups were dropped; older backups that contain it restore fine.)

- Studies are always fetched with comments (`comments=true`), whatever the checkbox says. If the saved studies were fetched before comments were supported, the Options page says to click Update studies.
- **Speech bubbles** (the comments box below the board was replaced by these at the user's request):
  - In the move list, a move that has a study comment, or a position/observation glyph (such as ± or =), gets a small speech-bubble icon just after its notation, after any move-quality glyph (e.g. "e4 ! 💬"). It appears once the move is made (by the user, the app, or a reveal), including the moves set up before a partway start.
  - Hovering over a bubble (or tapping it on a touch screen, or focusing it with the keyboard) pops up the comment, in a box with the same soft blue background as the bubbles (#6f90b4) and white text, on the practice page and in the Studies page's move tree alike (other pop-ups, such as the opening name and the hover boards, keep the dark grey background): the move in bold, then any position/observation glyphs with their names (e.g. "3… Bc5 = Equal position"), then the comment text, e.g. "Giuoco Piano". Clicking a bubble doesn't step the move list to that move.
  - **Color:** all bubbles, the intro bubble too, are a soft, muted blue (#6f90b4; a paler #9bb8d6 on hover), so comments are noticeable but quiet. (They were grey with only the newest in blue, then Lichess's bright blue #3692e7 everywhere, until the user asked for a lighter shade; "lighter" was taken to mean softer, as elsewhere on this dark page.)
  - **The newest bubble** pops (a brief grow-and-glow) once when it first appears, so a new comment isn't missed.
  - **The chapter's introductory comment:** a bubble just after the chapter name at the top of the panel.
- Comment text is shown without the arrows, circles and evaluations Lichess embeds in comments.
- Only the glyph symbol is shown, not its name; hovering over a glyph (in the move list, the move tree or a hint message) shows its Lichess name in the same soft blue pop-up with white text as comments (it was first the browser's own tooltip); the bubble's pop-up names position and observation glyphs in full. ### Single positions

- A **single position** tests one move of the user's: the position before it is set up instantly, without sounds; the earlier moves appear in the move list (and can be stepped back through), their comments get bubbles, and only that move is tested; the rest of the line isn't played. Single positions come from the Practice page's Positions choice.
- The same position is never tested twice in a row when there's another to choose.
- The position is named in the panel's heading, under the chapter name (the position reached, as for lines). (There used to be a second line naming it again, e.g. "Positions: Italian Game: Giuoco Piano, 4. c3 ♘f6"; the user removed that line, 6 October 2026.) Tries, hints and comments work as usual.
- After a right answer (including after wrong tries or a hint), the status says "Correct." and the app waits for **Next position** (or Enter), as at the end of a line (the user's decision in the UI clean-up; the next position used to follow by itself after about a second, except after a reveal).
- Each single position counts as one try of that move in its line (see Progress tracking).
- (Before the Positions page's buttons, "Test single positions" and "Partway through" were choices of "Where lines start" on the Studies page, with single positions only from move 5 on; they were removed.)

### Skip

- A **Skip** button next to Hint (the user's decision in the UI clean-up; it replaced **End line here**, which stopped a line and showed its summary, once End practice also recorded the moves answered). Skip goes straight on to the next line or position (with nothing else to practise, back to the chooser).
- The moves answered so far count as usual. The move the user is on counts only if they had already tried a move or used a hint on it; otherwise it isn't counted. A skipped line isn't a play-through (no line time), and it doesn't count in the "done" number.
- The Help page explains it under Practicing.

### Wrong moves

- The user can try as many times as they like; there's no limit and no automatic reveal. After a wrong try that isn't in the study, the piece goes back and the status says, **one fact per line** (the user's wording, 6 October 2026): **"Not your preferred move"**; then **"#3 masters move (played 1.2k times)"** for a masters move (see isMasterMove: among the top N master moves and played in 10% or more of master games there), otherwise **"Not a masters move"**; then **"#4 Lichess move (played 23k times)"** if it's among the Lichess games' saved moves there (the 10 most played plus the studies' own), otherwise **"Not played in Lichess games"**. ### Move popularity (Lichess database)

- **Automatic, in the background:** move statistics download by themselves, with no button: after **Update studies** (and when the studies are first fetched), and whenever the app opens, so a download that was interrupted resumes. Only positions without statistics are fetched; if nothing is missing, nothing is said. Update studies itself still finishes straight away. (History: at first they downloaded automatically after every refresh; then only with a "Refresh move frequency" button, later "Download move frequencies", in its own Options page box; the user then asked for automatic downloads again, without the button.) Move statistics download first, then Masters statistics.
- **Every position reached in the studies** is downloaded: every move and variation, including alternative moves' variations and each line's final position (earlier only positions where a move is played on the practiced lines were; the rest were added for the position count on the hover boards). For each, it's how often each move is played in the Lichess games database: all rated Lichess games, all ratings and time controls, not the Masters database. It uses Lichess's Opening Explorer (`explorer.lichess.org/lichess`), which requires the stored Lichess token (with no token, move statistics wait until one is saved; saving a token restarts the downloads).
- **What's saved for each position:** the Opening Explorer's answer **exactly as Lichess sends it** (`white`, `draws`, `black` for the games reaching the position; `moves`, a list with each move's `uci`, `san`, `averageRating` (the average rating of the players who made the move), `white`, `draws`, `black` and anything else Lichess includes; `topGames` and `recentGames`, empty because none are requested; and `opening` with its `eco` and `name`, or none for an unnamed position). The only thing dropped is moves beyond the **10 most played** (Lichess lists them most played first), except that any other move the studies have at that position (the user's main moves, opponent replies and alternative moves) is kept, so a rarer repertoire move still has its count and doesn't show 0%. Moves are matched ignoring + and #. Two fields are added: `v` (the format, 3) and `at` (when it was downloaded, used when merging a backup). A position no game reaches, found without asking Lichess, is saved in the same shape with zero games and `skipped`. The request still asks for up to 100 moves so the studies' rarer moves can be found. Nothing new is displayed yet; the position count and live ECO code (future improvements) will use it. (An interim version saved a reshaped copy, with moves keyed by name and `averageRating` renamed; the user asked for Lichess's own shape.)
- Positions saved before this (totals only) were treated as missing, so the next download fetched them all again with the details; until then their percentages still showed.
- Only positions that haven't been downloaded before are fetched; each position is fetched once even if it's in several lines. Results are saved in the browser (IndexedDB) and kept across visits.
- Requests are made one at a time, with no pause between them (there's no batch endpoint, so one request per position is the minimum). If Lichess answers "too many requests", the app waits a minute and carries on.
- **Most useful first:** positions in the selected chapters where the opponent has several replies come first (they drive "Prefer most common moves"), then the rest of the selected chapters, then everything else; within each group, shallower positions come before deeper ones.
- **Skipping positions nobody reaches:** if every known way into a position is a move that no Lichess game played (according to the statistics already downloaded for the position before it), the position is marked "no games" without a request. This is a close approximation: a different move order could occasionally reach such a position. The finished message says how many were skipped, e.g. "Move statistics downloaded for 1344 positions (380 skipped because no Lichess game reaches them)."
- A progress line in the Options page's Move frequency section (it was first under the header on every page) shows "Downloading move statistics: 120 of 850 positions", then "Move statistics downloaded for N positions." for a few seconds. Practice can continue while it runs; positions not downloaded yet have no percentages. If the connection fails, the line says so and that the rest downloads the next time the app opens or Update studies is pressed; if the token is rejected, it says to replace it on the Options page, after which the download continues. Closing the page stops the download; it resumes the next time the app opens.
- **Download order** (the same whatever is ticked, across all studies): (1) branch points (positions where the opponent has several replies), then (2) the rest of the chapters' main lines, then (3) everything else; within each group by move number (move 1 first). (It used to put the ticked chapters first.) A deep branch point may be fetched before the positions leading to it, so the "no game reaches it" shortcut can't always apply and a few extra requests are made; accepted.
- **Masters statistics:** for the same positions, the app also downloads Lichess's **Masters** database (over-the-board games between players rated 2200+ FIDE, from 1952): `explorer.lichess.org/masters?fen=…&moves=100&topGames=0`, also with the Lichess token. The answer is saved **exactly as Lichess sends it with the full move list** (masters positions usually have only a handful of moves), but **without the top master games** (none are requested, so `topGames` comes back empty), plus `v` (1) and `at`. It's stored separately from the Lichess games data (IndexedDB key `masters`), downloads automatically in the background after the Lichess games statistics (same order, same "no game reaches it" shortcut: "skipped because no master game reaches them"), has its own status and progress lines in the Move frequency box ("Masters statistics: 850 of 860 positions downloaded."), is included in backups and merged on restore (the newer copy of each position wins), and gets its own backup suggestion when complete. - **Backup suggestion:** when a download finishes and every position has its statistics (not when nothing was missing, or when it stopped part-way), the Options page suggests a backup, under Update studies (see Messages about fetching from Lichess): "Move statistics are complete. Save a backup so you won't need to download them again?" (or "Masters statistics are complete. …") with **Save backup** and **Not now** buttons.

### Spaced repetition and its sliders

- **Practice lines** practises every line of the chapters ticked on the Studies page, filtered by **Main lines only**, each from move 1, including lines never tried. **Practice positions** practises every move of the user's in the ticked chapters (once each), filtered by **Positions from main lines only**, each as a single position, including moves never tried. Neither uses the table's count and accuracy drop-downs (the user's decision). (These were first the Lines and Positions pages' "Practice all lines/positions" buttons.)
- The user asked for a bias towards new and untested items, items with low accuracy and items not tested in a while, and across all of these towards more frequent positions. *Claude's algorithm, accepted by the user:*
  - Before each item, every item gets a **priority = frequency × need**, and the next item is picked at random with chances in proportion to it (so the order is never fully predictable, and priorities reflect the answers just given).
  - **Need:** 6 for an item never tried (a line none of whose moves have been tried). Otherwise **weakness × due**: weakness = 1 + 2 × (1 − accuracy), from 1 at 100% to 3 at 0%; due = days since last tested ÷ the item's review interval, kept between 0.1 and 3 (3 when the last test time isn't known); the interval is 2^(7 × accuracy) days: 1 day at 0%, about 11 days at 50%, 128 days at 100%.
  - **Accuracy:** a position's move accuracy; a line's line accuracy, with its untried moves counting as 0%. **Last tested:** a position's last-tried time; a line's last play-through from move 1 to the end.
  - **Frequency:** 0.2 to 1, by the rated Lichess games reaching the position (for a line, its final position), on a log scale up to the most frequent item being practised; 0.6 for an item whose statistics haven't downloaded.
  - E.g. a 100% position tested yesterday has need 0.1; a 50% position untested for a month 5.3; a new one 6; a 0% one never timed 9.
- **The sliders** (the user's design): four sliders, 0% to 100%, shared by lines and positions, under **Spaced repetition focus**: **Frequency**, **Accuracy** (low accuracy), **Last played** (not tested for a while) and **Untried**. The defaults give exactly the priority above. Moving Frequency and Accuracy to 100% and the others to 0% practises (almost) only common positions with low accuracy. *Claude's design, accepted by the user:* each slider is a power on its part of the priority: 0% → power 0 (ignored), 50% (the default) → power 1 (as above), 100% → power 6, so the items highest in it take almost every pick (in between, the power rises evenly: 25% → 0.5, 75% → 3.5). **Untried** works on the 6 that untried items get: at 0% untried items never come up, and at 100% they get every pick until none is left. Sliders change chances, not rules: apart from Untried at 0% or 100%, every item can still come up.
- **No line saying what's being practised** (the user's request, 6 October 2026: it should be obvious): the panel shows just the study and chapter, then the position reached. (It used to add a line, "Lines" or "Positions: <position>", earlier "All lines" / "All positions: …", with a count such as "· 7 done".)
- The Help page's "How practice is chosen" card explains it, with the numbers in a collapsed section.

### No immediate repeats

- The same line is never picked twice in a row when any other line is available. This always applies.
- If the selection has only one line, it repeats.
- The last line is remembered until the page is closed or reloaded, so the rule also applies after going back to the selection screen and starting again.

## Progress tracking

- Results are recorded **per move, at each position in a chapter**, and kept between visits (stored in the browser, like the token and selection).
- A **line** is one path through a chapter from the start to the end of a variation, following the main line at every user move and any variation at opponent moves. It is identified by its study, chapter, and move sequence.
- **One record per position:** each of the user's moves has one record per point in the chapter's move tree, identified by the chapter plus the moves leading to and including it. Every line that passes through that point shares the record, so getting 3. Bc4 right in one line counts for every line that continues from it. The same board position in a **different chapter**, or reached by a **different move order**, keeps its own record (the user may play differently in different contexts). This also applies to the times recorded (when each position was last tried, and line times). The user confirmed that history is **not** to be merged across chapters.
- History: at first, records were kept per line (the same move in two lines, even with the same moves before it, was recorded separately). The user asked to drop line scoring and keep only the scoring of moves at a position, which also gives the move tree one score per move. Existing per-line records were merged into the position records when the app was next opened: each line's results for a move joined that position's record, keeping the most recent 10 (older backups are converted the same way when restored).
- For each of the user's moves, the app keeps its **last 10 attempts**. Older attempts drop off. Each attempt saves:
  - **what the user did, in order:** every move tried (wrong moves, alternative moves marked or not, and the correct move at the end if it was played) and the moments the Hint and Show correct move buttons were pressed, e.g. `["d4", "Bc4", "hint", "show", "e4"]`; a correct first try is just `["e4"]`; an attempt cut short by Skip or End practice has no correct move at the end;
  - its **points**, 0–10 (see Grading an attempt).
- **Grading an attempt:** each attempt starts at **10 points**; finding the correct move on the first try keeps all 10. Every step before the correct move costs points, each time it happens (repeats cost again):
  - an **unmarked alternative move** from the study: **−2** by default (the **Alternative move** penalty);
  - a **masters move**: **−2** by default (the **Masters move** penalty). A masters move is one **within the top N** master moves in that position (N chosen on the Options page, top 1 to top 5, default **top 5**; moves played equally often share a rank, so a tie at N counts) **and** played in **10% or more** of master games there (from the Masters statistics). E.g. with top 5, if only 3 moves reach 10%, only those 3 count; a 5th move at 6% doesn't. A move that is both an unmarked alternative and a masters move costs the alternative's penalty, once. (It was "more than 10%" with no top-N limit until the user made it configurable.)
  - an **alternative marked ?!, ? or ??**: **−5** by default (the **Error** penalty; the study's marking wins over a masters move);
  - **any other wrong move**: **−5** by default (the **Error** penalty). (Errors were −10 until the user lowered them to −5; the hint stayed at −10.)
  - the **Hint**: **−10** by default (the **Hint** penalty); **Show correct move** costs the same again, so with the default the score is already 0.
  - **The penalties are set on the Options page**, in a card titled **"Error penalties reducing move accuracy"**: one drop-down each for Alternative move, Masters move, Error (which covers marked alternatives too) and Hint (which covers Show correct move), each **0%, 10% … 100%** of an attempt's score (1 point = 10%), defaults 20%, 20%, 50%, 100%; and, under a small heading **Masters moves** at the end of the card, a drop-down **"What counts as a masters move"**, Top 1 … Top 5, default Top 5 (it was between Masters move and Error until the UI clean-up). Each drop-down has a short grey explanation after its name. Changing any of them **re-scores all saved attempts straight away**, so the highlighting, the Studies page percentages and the Positions page reflect it at once. They're remembered and included in backups. *Claude's choices, accepted by the user:* the card is the first on the Options page; a line in progress keeps the points already worked out for the moves played so far in it.
  - The floor is **0**. So an attempt scores 10, 8, 6 or 4 when the only slips were reasonable moves, 5 after one real mistake, 0 after two, and 0 after the hint. If a position's masters statistics haven't downloaded yet, no move there counts as a master move.
- **Points aren't stored, only what was done:** each attempt is saved as what the user tried and when. Its points are worked out from that, with the current rules and data (e.g. masters statistics downloaded since, or alternatives added in Update studies), when it's recorded, when the app opens, after restoring a backup, and when a penalty changes. A score can therefore change between sessions. (Points used to be saved with each attempt and recalculated each session anyway; they were dropped from storage in the data clean-up of 4 October 2026, the user's decision on Claude's suggestion.)
- **When each line was played:** for each line, the app saves when it was played through to its end, the **last 10 times** (oldest dropped), used for Last tested on the Lines page and by All (spaced repetition). A line's time is the time of the **last move attempted** in it. A line counts as played through only when it's played all the way from move 1 to the end; a line skipped or stopped with End practice, and single positions, don't record a time for the line, only for the moves played (see When each position was last tried). (At first a partway line that reached its end counted too; the user changed it, as simpler.) Nothing is recorded for plays before this was added. The times are dropped for lines that no longer exist after Update studies, and they're included in backups (a backup without them restores fine).
- **When each move was tried:** every attempt at one of the user's moves is saved with its time (the user's decision in the data clean-up of 4 October 2026; at first only a single "last tried" time per move was kept, after the user first decided against times per attempt). A position's last-tried time is its newest attempt's. Times are recorded whenever an attempt is recorded: lines played to the end, lines ended early (for the move being tried), End practice and single positions. Attempts from before per-attempt times have none ("–" for Last tested if none of a move's attempts has one), except that each move's last attempt took over its old "last tried" time. Dropped for moves no longer in the studies after Update studies; included in backups. It's shown as Last tested in the positions table and used by All (spaced repetition).
- The Help page explains both (Your statistics, "When lines and positions were last practiced"): a line counts only when played all the way through, from move 1 to the end; a position counts whenever it's attempted, so it can be more recent than any line through it (the user asked for this to be in the Help).
- A move's **move accuracy** (in the code, its score) is the **weighted** average points of its last 10 attempts (or however many there are), as a percentage of 10. Recent attempts count more: the most recent is weighted 100%, the one before 90%, then 80% … down to 10% for the 10th most recent; the score is Σ(weight × points) ÷ Σ(weights). E.g. attempts of 0, 8, 10, 10 (oldest to newest): (10×1.0 + 10×0.9 + 8×0.8 + 0×0.7) ÷ 3.4 ≈ 7.5, i.e. 75% (a plain average would be 70%). The hover board's "Average 7.5 of 10" is this weighted average. (It was a plain average until the user asked for recent attempts to count more.) Percentages are rounded down, so a score just under a boundary keeps the lower color. The score drives everything as before: the highlighting, the Studies page percentages, and the weights for "Prefer lines I miss" and Test single positions (a move's wrong rate being 1 − score).
- History: at first each attempt was just right (first try, no hint) or wrong, saved as 1 or as the list of wrong moves played, and unmarked alternatives weren't counted. The user asked for this points system. Existing results were converted when the app next opened: right became `[correct move]` with 10 points; wrong became the saved wrong moves (or `["hint"]` for an attempt that was only a hint or Show correct move), scored with the new rules (a hint used after a wrong move wasn't recorded then, so those may score a little high until they age out).
- **Highlighting** (a chapter's move tree and the Studies page scores): the move text stays in the normal color, and only moves that need work are highlighted, with **one violet background for anything below 100%** (the user's decision, 6 October 2026: simpler, and the shades no longer helped; 100% and untried are plain). The same violet marks studies, chapters, lines, positions and the practice move list's moves that lost points. In a chapter's move tree, the violet sits behind the move **and its glyphs together**, e.g. "♘f3 !" (the user's request; the practice move list already did this). (It used to come in three strengths: faint for 90–99%, medium for 70–89%, strong for 69% or less.) *Claude's choice, accepted by the user:* the violet is between the old medium and strong tints. Every move's text, the user's (right, weak or never tried) and the opponent's, is in the standard Lichess text color, like any move on Lichess; the only exception is a move the study marks with a move-quality glyph (! ? !! ?? !? ?! □), whose text takes the glyph's Lichess color. (For a while right moves were brighter white, untried moves grey with a dotted underline and the opponent's moves grey; the user asked for the standard color throughout.)
- Why violet: on Lichess and Chess.com, red and amber on a move mean the move is bad, but here the highlighted move is the correct one that the user keeps missing. Violet isn't used by chess sites for move quality, so the two meanings don't clash. (The first version colored the move text white / green / amber / red; the user asked for a different approach and chose background highlighting of weak moves only.) The small board's arrows keep green for the correct move and red for wrong moves, since there those colors do mean right and wrong.
- A play-through of a line records a try for each of the user's moves actually played; and a single position records one try of that move.
- A line stopped part way with Skip or End practice records the moves answered so far (see Skip and End practice). Going to another page doesn't stop a line (Practice returns to it). *Claude's choice, accepted by the user:* a line dropped because the studies were refreshed in the middle of it records nothing, since its moves may no longer be in the study; nor does a line left when the app is closed.
- The earlier per-line stats (plays and misses per line, plus review dates) were cleared once when this was built, since they don't say which move was missed. Older backups with those stats restore their settings, but their stats are left out (the message says so).
- After a refresh from Lichess, records for positions no longer in any line are dropped; the rest keep their records.
- The Studies page's average for a chapter or study counts each tried position once.

## Terms: move accuracy, line accuracy, errors and position reached

- The app uses two terms, consistently everywhere the user reads them (the user's standing rule):
  - **Move accuracy:** how well one of the user's moves is known, as a percentage. For one attempt it's the attempt's points × 10, e.g. "**80% move accuracy**" on the practice hover board (it used to say "80% accuracy this time"). Over time it's the weighted average of the move's last 10 attempts (see Progress tracking), e.g. "**75% move accuracy (last 4 tries)**" on the move tree's hover board (it used to say "Average 7.5 of 10 (last 4 tries)").
  - **Line accuracy:** the average move accuracy of the user's moves in one completed line, e.g. "**92% line accuracy**" under "Line complete." (it used to say "overall accuracy").
  - **Error** and **mistake** are different (the user's standing rule): an **error** is any move the user plays that isn't the one in their study (their preferred line), and the hint; errors are what cost move accuracy (the hover boards' "Errors: …" rows, "No errors." after a clean line, the Options page's "Error penalties reducing move accuracy"). **Mistake** is reserved for moves a study marks as a mistake (?), blunder (??) or dubious (?!) (the user decided to count ?! as a mistake, consistently through the app; the Help page explains both terms under Your statistics), e.g. "♗b5? is one of your alternative moves, marked as a mistake" and "Prefer opponent's mistakes". (The move tree's hover board used to say "Wrong: Bc4 ×2, d3", now "Errors: ♗c4 ×2, d3"; the Options card was "Mistake penalties".)
  - **Position reached:** how many times a position was reached in rated Lichess games (from the move statistics), shown with the same short counts everywhere (see Naming positions): "**Position reached 23k times**" on the hover boards (the position after the move) and the column "**Position reached (times)**" on the Positions page (the position where it's the user's move; the column used to be called "Lichess games").
- Where they appear: the practice and move-tree hover boards, the end of a line, the positions table (title "Frequent positions with low move accuracy", column "Move accuracy"), the Studies page's percentages (hover text "Average move accuracy over the last 10 attempts"; it used to say "Accuracy over the last 10 attempts"), the Options page's error penalties text and the Help page. The Help page and the rest of this spec say "move accuracy" where they used to say a move's "score". *Claude's choices, accepted by the user:* the Studies page hover text adds "Average" because a study's or chapter's percentage averages several moves; the move tree's hover board shows a whole percentage, rounded down like the Studies page.

## Naming positions

- **Counts of games** (the positions table's Position reached (times) column and the hover board's "Position reached … times") are shortened the same way everywhere: up to 999 as they are, then thousands ("23k"), millions with one decimal ("1.4M"), and billions with one decimal ("1.8bn", the user's request, instead of e.g. "1846.5M"). ## Technology

- **Version control:** the project folder is a local git repository (no GitHub or upload), set up at the user's request; each agreed change can be committed as a snapshot and undone by going back to an earlier one. The first commit recorded the app, spec, project notes and the natural-language archive.

- **Delivery:** a single HTML file (`trainer.html`) on the user's computer, opened directly in the browser (`file://`). No server, no build step.
- **Lichess API:** called directly from the page; the Lichess API allows cross-origin browser requests.
- **Libraries:** chessops and chessground (JS and CSS) are loaded from the jsDelivr CDN via ES module imports. The app needs internet to reach Lichess anyway, so this adds no new requirement.
- **PGN parsing:** use [chessops](https://github.com/niklasf/chessops), the library Lichess itself uses. Its `chessops/pgn` module parses study PGN into a move tree that keeps all variations, and its move logic validates the user's moves.
- **Font:** Noto Sans from jsDelivr (`@fontsource/noto-sans`), plus Noto Sans Symbols 2 (`@fontsource/noto-sans-symbols-2`) for the chess piece symbols; only the subset containing the symbols is downloaded.
- **Move statistics:** Lichess Opening Explorer API (`explorer.lichess.org/lichess`), called from the page with the Lichess token.
- **Sounds:** Lichess's SFX move and capture sounds, served by jsDelivr from the `lichess-org/lila` GitHub repository at a pinned commit.
- **Board UI:** use [chessground](https://github.com/lichess-org/chessground), Lichess's board library, which pairs with chessops. The user makes moves by dragging or clicking pieces on it. It uses Lichess's default look (brown board, cburnett pieces); no other board or piece styles are offered.

## Possible future improvements

- **Ideas from research into what 1400+ and 1800+ players need** (30 September 2026; suggested by Claude after the user asked; not yet discussed in detail, so each needs the user's decisions before building). In Claude's suggested order of value:
  1. **Your Lichess games compared with your repertoire** (both levels; highest value): fetch the user's recent Lichess games with the stored token and, for each, find where the user left the repertoire (a forgotten move: those come up first in practice) and where the opponent left it (a gap in the studies, listed by how often it actually happened).

## Ideas for a separate app

Not part of this app; recorded so they aren't lost.

- **AI game reviewer that explains moves** (30 September 2026): an app to evaluate a whole game, combining a chess engine (Stockfish or Lichess's cloud evaluations) with an AI (Claude) that explains *why* a position is better for one side and why a move is good or bad, in human terms (pawn structure, king safety, piece activity, plans), rather than only giving numbers and engine lines.
  - How it would work (Claude's outline): the engine supplies the facts (evaluation, best moves, the refutation of a mistake); code works out the concrete board features (material, pawn structure, king safety, open files, loose pieces); the AI turns these into an explanation; and the engine checks each claim (e.g. that a stated threat really works), discarding explanations that don't hold up. Where an evaluation rests on deep tactics with no human-sized reason, it should say so instead of inventing one, and small differences between good moves aren't worth explaining.
  - Why separate (the user's decision): explanations matter most at a game's turning points, mostly in the middlegame, rather than in prepared openings, whose ideas the study comments already cover; a game review has a natural shape (find the turning points, explain only those), which also keeps the number and cost of AI questions low; and it keeps this app free of an API key, an engine download and per-question costs.
  - A possible link: finding where a game left the repertoire is close to this app's future idea of comparing your Lichess games with your repertoire, so the two could share that step.
