Getting started

  1. On Lichess, create a personal API token with the Read private studies (study:read) permission.
  2. Paste it into the app. It fetches all your studies, private ones included, and remembers the token.
  3. When you change your studies on Lichess, use Update studies on the System page.
  4. How often each move is played on Lichess (and in master games), and Lichess's evaluations, download automatically in the background after your studies are fetched or updated (and resume when you open the app). The first time can take a while; a line on the System page shows the progress, and you can practice meanwhile. When the move statistics are complete, the app suggests saving a backup.
What the app stores, and where

Everything stays in this browser: your token, your studies, your stats and your settings. Nothing is sent anywhere except Lichess (to fetch studies, move statistics and evaluations). Stats don't move to another browser or computer by themselves; use a backup for that (see below).

Choosing what to practice

  • Tick studies or individual chapters in the list, or All. Click ▸ to show a study's chapters.
  • Each study shows how many chapters it has, and each chapter how many lines and alternative moves, e.g. "5 lines · 2 alternative moves". A line is one path through a chapter that you're quizzed on: your main move at each of your turns, and every reply your study gives the opponent. An alternative move is another move for you that your study has at one of your turns, for example 3. Bb5 where the main move is 3. Bc4. Alternative moves aren't tested, because only the main move counts as correct. If you play one, the app tells you: a move your study marks ?, ?? or ?! is shown as an error, and an unmarked one gets "also in your study". In a chapter's move tree they're shown in italics.
  • Click the first ▸ next to a chapter to see its moves as a tree, as on Lichess: the main line runs along the top, and a small + after a move opens its variations underneath (− closes them). The second ▸ next to a chapter opens every variation at once (▾ closes them all). Moves you need to work on are highlighted in violet (see Your statistics), alternative moves for you (which aren't tested), and the moves after them, are in italics, and moves your study marks with a glyph such as ! or ? take the glyph's color. The end of each line shows its Lichess evaluation. Hover over a move to see the small board.
  • Save favorites remembers the chapters ticked now; Recall favorites ticks exactly those chapters again.
  • To hide a study you no longer use, click the eye at the top (next to the favorites buttons): hidden studies appear in grey and each study gets its own eye after its name. Click a study's eye to hide it (a line through it) or unhide it. Click the top eye again to put hidden studies out of sight. Hidden studies aren't practiced.
  • The small circle after each chapter's name shows which side you play: white or black (the board orientation you set on Lichess). Each chapter also shows, once you've played it, how often you get its moves right (e.g. "82%" (hover over it for an explanation)), highlighted the same way as your moves (see Your statistics).
  • Opponent's moves: Main line only (the opponent always plays the chapter's main move), Prefer most common moves (replies played most often on Lichess come up most; until their statistics have downloaded, replies without them are treated as equally likely, so it works like All moves equally for them), Prefer opponent's mistakes (replies you marked ?, ?? or ?! come up about 4 times as often), Prefer lines I miss (the default: lines with moves you often get wrong come up more), or All moves equally (every reply in your study is equally likely).

Practicing

  • You can visit any other page in the middle of a line; press Practice to return to it where you left off. If you change the chapters, Opponent's moves or Where lines start (or refresh your studies) in the meantime, Practice starts afresh instead.
  • Press Practice at the top of any page, then Start practice, to begin (or press Start practice on the Studies page to begin straight away).
  • Four small icons sit at the top right of the panel, next to the chapter name: the violet box (missed moves highlighted during the line), the speech bubble (annotations during the line), the eye (chapter names during the line) and the bell (sounds). Click one to switch it; a line through an icon means that thing is off. The first three only affect what you see while playing a line: once the line is over, missed moves are highlighted and annotations and names are always shown, and your setting carries on to the next line. Hovering over the first three says what they're set to now, e.g. "Show annotations during the line" or, with the line through it, "Hide annotations during the line"; hovering over the bell says what a click will do ("Turn sounds on / off").
  • Moves are written with piece symbols, as on Lichess: ♘ knight, ♗ bishop, ♖ rook, ♕ queen, ♔ king (so ♗c4 means Bc4). Pawn moves have no symbol.
  • You play the side at the bottom of the board; the app plays the other side's moves. Drag or click pieces to move.
  • Only the study's main-line move counts as correct. You can keep trying as long as you like.
  • If your study has other moves for you at that point: one marked ?, ?? or ?! is shown as an error, with the opponent's reply from your study, and counts as a wrong try; an unmarked one just gets "also in your study, but it isn't the main move" and doesn't count against you.
  • Hint: circles the piece to move. The button then becomes Show correct move, which draws an arrow for the right move; you then play it yourself. Either counts as getting the move wrong.
  • Lines are always played to the end. To stop sooner, press End line here on your move: you get the summary straight away, and the moves you've played count as usual (the move you're on counts only if you'd already had a wrong try or a hint on it). It's just for this time; the next time the line comes up it's played in full.
  • The study's glyphs (such as !) appear in the move list; hover over a glyph to see its name (e.g. Good move). A move with a study comment gets a small speech bubble: hover over it (or tap it) to read the comment. Bubbles are blue so they stand out, and a new one pops when it appears, so you don't miss it. The chapter's introductory comment is a bubble next to the chapter name. After a wrong move, Hint also shows what your study says about that move. Click the speech-bubble icon to hide all this while you play a line, or show it again; a line through it means annotations are hidden during lines. They always appear once the line is over, and on the Studies page.
  • Click a move in the move list, or use ◀ ▶ or the arrow keys, to look back at earlier positions. Hovering over any move already played shows the small board with its Lichess share and, for your moves, how you did on it this time.
  • When a line ends, the moves you got wrong this time get a violet background in the move list, and the status says e.g. "Line complete. Evaluation +0.4", with Lichess's evaluation of the final position. Hover over any move in the move list to see how often it's played in Lichess games and, for your moves, how you did this time ("Correct first try" or "Wrong this time", with red arrows for any wrong moves you played). Press Next line (or Enter) to continue.
  • Position evaluation: after each line, the summary shows Lichess's cloud evaluation of the final position in the status, e.g. "Line complete. Evaluation +0.4" (if you end a line early, the summary shows only the moves you played, with no evaluation). As on Lichess, it's from White's side: + means White is better, − means Black is better, and #3 / #-3 means White / Black mates in 3. Hover over it to see how deeply Lichess analyzed it. Lichess only has evaluations for positions in its cloud database (most common opening positions), so some line ends have none. They download automatically in the background; they're saved, and a chapter's move tree on the Studies page shows each at the end of its line. If a line ends without one, the summary fetches it on the spot.
  • At the top of the Studies page, choose From the start, Partway through or Test single positions. Partway through jumps to a random point in each line and quizzes you from there to the end; the moves before that point are in the move list. Test single positions tests just one of your moves, then moves to a different position; each counts as one try of that move, and moves you often get wrong come up more.
  • To make it harder, click the eye icon (Hide chapter names during the line) to hide the study and chapter names (a line through the eye means they're hidden; "Chapter" and "Study" show instead). The chapter's introductory comment is hidden with them. They always appear when the line ends, and the eye stays as you left it for the next line. Click it again to show them.

Your statistics

The app scores each of your moves separately, not whole lines, so you can see exactly which moves you know and which you don't.

  • Right: you played the move on your first try, with no hint.
  • Wrong: any wrong try (including a move your study marks ?, ?? or ?!), a hint, or Show correct move. The wrong moves you actually played are remembered too.
  • Not counted: a move your study has as an unmarked alternative (you just try again), and any line you leave unfinished.
  • Each move keeps only its last 10 tries. Its score is the percentage right over those tries, e.g. 7 right out of 10 is 70%. With fewer than 10 tries, it's out of however many there are.
  • Highlighting: moves you know (100%) look plain. Moves that need work get a violet background, stronger the weaker they are: 90–99% faint, 70–89% medium, 69% or less strong. Moves you haven't tried yet, and the opponent's moves, look plain too. As on Lichess, a move's text is colored only when the study gives it a glyph (e.g. ! green, ? orange). (Violet is used so it isn't confused with the red and amber that chess sites use for bad moves; your highlighted moves are the right moves, ones you keep missing.)
What counts as a try
  • Playing a line from the start: one try for each of your moves in it.
  • Partway through: one try for each of your moves from the starting point on; the moves before it aren't counted.
  • Test single positions: one try of the move tested.
  • Leaving a line before it ends (for example when Practice starts afresh after you change the selection) records nothing.
Which lines share a score

Each score belongs to one of your moves at one point in a chapter: the chapter plus the moves leading to it. Every line that passes through that point shares the score, so getting 3. Bc4 right in one line counts for all the lines that continue from it. The same position in a different chapter, or reached by a different move order, keeps its own score.

Where you see your statistics
  • Studies page: each study and chapter shows the average score of the moves you've tried in it, e.g. "82%" (hover over it for an explanation), highlighted the same way. Nothing is shown until you've tried a move there.
  • End of a line: the moves you got wrong this time, in the order you played them, e.g. "5. Bc4 and 5. d3 instead of 5. c3", with "(shown)" or "(hint: piece)" if you used those. Below that, the line's moves colored by their updated scores.
  • A chapter's move tree (click the ▸ next to a chapter on the Studies page): every move of the chapter, with yours highlighted by score and, once saved, the Lichess evaluation at the end of each line.
  • Small board: in a chapter's move tree, and in the practice move list once a line is over, hover over (or tap) any move. For your moves it shows the position you had to answer, with a green arrow for the correct move, and a red arrow for each wrong move you've played in the last 10 tries, and above it the move with how often it's played in Lichess games, e.g. "5. c3 (Lichess games 45%)" (once the statistics have downloaded), then e.g. "7 of last 10 correct" and "Wrong: Bc4 ×2, d3". For the opponent's moves it shows the position after the move, with the move as a white arrow, or a red arrow if your study marks it ?, ?? or ?!.
When statistics are dropped
  • After Update studies, scores for lines that no longer exist in your studies are dropped; everything else is kept.
  • Statistics live only in this browser. Use a backup (System page) to keep them safe or move them.

How lines are chosen

  • With Prefer lines I miss (and Main line only), lines with moves you often get wrong come up more often, and new lines are introduced fairly soon. The other Opponent's moves choices ignore your statistics.
  • The same line is never given twice in a row when there's another to choose.
How the weighting works

Each of your moves gets a weight of 1 + 4 × how often you get it wrong (over its last 10 tries): a move you always get right has weight 1, one you always get wrong has weight 5, and one you haven't tried yet has weight 3. A line's chance of coming up is its weakest move's weight, not the average, so one bad move is enough to bring a line back. For example, a line where every move is 100% has weight 1, but if one move is at 50% the line has weight 3, so it comes up three times as often. In Test single positions, the move tested within the line is picked by the same weights.

System and backups

  • System: refreshing studies and move statistics, downloading position evaluations, your Lichess token, and backups.
  • Backup: Save backup saves a file with your stats, settings and downloaded Lichess data (move frequencies and evaluations, so they needn't be downloaded again) (in Chrome and Edge you choose where; Safari and Firefox put it in your download folder, unless you set them to ask where to save downloads). Restore backup loads it again, on another browser or after clearing your browser. It never includes your token.

Lichess studies

Update studies downloads all your Lichess studies again, private ones included, so changes you've made on Lichess (new chapters, moves, variations or comments) appear here. Your statistics are kept, except for moves that are no longer in your studies.

Move frequency

How often each move is played in all rated Lichess games, shown on the small boards when you hover over a move, and in the Masters database (over-the-board games between players rated 2200+ FIDE), saved for later use. The statistics for positions that don't have them yet download automatically in the background after Update studies (and resume when you open the app); you can keep practicing meanwhile.

Position evaluation

Lichess's evaluation of the final position of each line (e.g. +0.4, from White's side), shown after each line and at the end of each line in a chapter's move tree. They come from Lichess's cloud database of positions it has already analyzed, so some positions have none. Missing ones download automatically in the background after Update studies (and resume when you open the app), and are saved.
