Getting started

  1. On Lichess, create a personal API token with the Read private studies (study:read) permission.
  2. Paste it into the app. It fetches all your studies, private ones included, and remembers the token.
  3. When you change your studies on Lichess, use Update studies on the Options page.
  4. How often each move is played on Lichess and in master games downloads automatically in the background after your studies are fetched or updated (and resume when you open the app). The first time can take a while; a line on the Options page shows the progress, and you can practice meanwhile. When the move statistics are complete, the app suggests saving a backup.
What the app stores, and where

Everything stays in this browser: your token, your studies, your stats and your settings. Nothing is sent anywhere except Lichess (to fetch studies and move statistics). Stats don't move to another browser or computer by themselves; use a backup for that (see below).

Choosing what to practice

  • Tick studies or individual chapters in the list, or All. Click ▸ to show a study's chapters.
  • Each study shows how many chapters it has, and each chapter how many lines and alternative moves, e.g. "5 lines · 2 alternative moves". A line is one path through a chapter that you're quizzed on: your main move at each of your turns, and every reply your study gives the opponent. An alternative move is another move for you that your study has at one of your turns, for example 3. Bb5 where the main move is 3. Bc4. Alternative moves aren't tested, because only the main move counts as correct. If you play one, the app tells you: a move your study marks ?, ?? or ?! is shown as an error, and an unmarked one gets "is one of your alternative moves". In a chapter's move tree they're shown in italics.
  • Click the first ▸ next to a chapter to see its moves as a tree, as on Lichess: the main line runs along the top, and a small + after a move opens its variations underneath (− closes them). The second ▸ next to a chapter opens every variation at once (▾ closes them all). Moves you need to work on are highlighted in violet (see Your statistics), alternative moves for you (which aren't tested), and the moves after them, are in italics, and moves your study marks with a glyph such as ! or ? take the glyph's color. Hover over a move to see the small board.
  • Save favorites remembers the chapters ticked now; Recall favorites ticks exactly those chapters again.
  • To hide a study you no longer use, click the eye at the top (next to the favorites buttons): hidden studies appear in grey and each study gets its own eye after its name. Click a study's eye to hide it (a line through it) or unhide it. Click the top eye again to put hidden studies out of sight. Hidden studies aren't practiced.
  • The small circle after each chapter's name shows which side you play: white or black (the board orientation you set on Lichess). Each chapter also shows, once you've played it, the average move accuracy of its moves (e.g. "82%"; hover over it for an explanation), highlighted the same way as your moves (see Your statistics).
  • Opponent's moves: Main line only (the opponent always plays the chapter's main move), Prefer most common moves (replies played most often on Lichess come up most; until their statistics have downloaded, replies without them are treated as equally likely, so it works like All moves equally for them), Prefer opponent's mistakes (replies you marked ?, ?? or ?! come up about 4 times as often), Prefer lines I miss (the default: lines with moves you often get wrong come up more), or All moves equally (every reply in your study is equally likely).

Practicing

  • You can visit any other page in the middle of a line; press Practice to return to it where you left off. If you change the chapters, Opponent's moves or Where lines start (or refresh your studies) in the meantime, Practice starts afresh instead.
  • Press Practice at the top of any page, then Start practice, to begin (or press Start practice on the Studies page to begin straight away).
  • The bell under the board turns move sounds on and off; a line through it means sounds are off. Hovering over it says what a click will do ("Turn sounds on / off").
  • Moves are written with piece symbols, as on Lichess: ♘ knight, ♗ bishop, ♖ rook, ♕ queen, ♔ king (so ♗c4 means Bc4). Pawn moves have no symbol.
  • You play the side at the bottom of the board; the app plays the other side's moves. Drag or click pieces to move.
  • Only the study's main-line move counts as correct. You can keep trying as long as you like.
  • If your study has other moves for you at that point: one marked ?, ?? or ?! is shown as an error, with the opponent's reply from your study, and costs an error's penalty; an unmarked one gets "♗c4 is one of your alternative moves" and costs an alternative's penalty (see Your statistics). Any other wrong move says how popular it is, e.g. "♕d3 is the #3 masters move but not your move" (a masters move, see Your statistics) or "♕d3 is the #4 Lichess move but not your move" (an error), or just "♕d3 is not your move".
  • Hint: circles the piece to move. The button then becomes Show correct move, which draws an arrow for the right move; you then play it yourself. Each costs the hint's penalty (by default 100%, so the move accuracy is 0% this time).
  • Lines are always played to the end. To stop sooner, press End line here on your move: you get the summary straight away, and the moves you've played count as usual (the move you're on counts only if you'd already tried a move or used Hint on it). It's just for this time; the next time the line comes up it's played in full.
  • The study's glyphs (such as !) appear in the move list; hover over a glyph to see its name (e.g. Good move). A move with a study comment gets a small speech bubble: hover over it (or tap it) to read the comment. Bubbles are blue so they stand out, and a new one pops when it appears, so you don't miss it. The heading above the moves shows the study and chapter (with the chapter's introductory comment as a bubble), then the position reached, named by its opening and the moves since, which updates as you play. After a wrong move, Hint also shows what your study says about that move.
  • Click a move in the move list, or use ◀ ▶ or the arrow keys, to look back at earlier positions. Hovering over any move already played shows the small board with its Lichess share and, for your moves, how you did on it this time.
  • A move you lose points on gets a violet background in the move list as soon as you've played it. When a line ends, the status says "Line complete.", then how it went, e.g. "3 of 6 moves correct" or "No mistakes." (a move is correct when you found it first time without a hint; hover over a violet-highlighted move to see what went wrong there) and "77% line accuracy" (the average move accuracy of your moves in the line). Hover over any move in the move list to see how many times the position was reached ("Position reached 23k times"), the move's share of all Lichess games and of master games and, for your moves, how you did this time (e.g. "80% move accuracy", then what you tried before the right move, such as "Errors: exd5, a3 (alternative), b3 (masters), hint", with a green arrow for the right move, blue arrows for alternatives and masters moves and red arrows for other wrong moves; "(alternative)" and "(masters)" mark the alternatives and masters moves, which by default cost less than errors). Press Next line (or Enter) to continue.
  • At the top of the Studies page, choose From the start, Partway through or Test single positions. Partway through jumps to a random point in each line and quizzes you from there to the end; the moves before that point are in the move list. Test single positions tests just one of your moves, then moves to a different position; each counts as one try of that move, and moves you often get wrong come up more.

Your statistics

The app measures the move accuracy of each of your moves separately (and a line accuracy each time you complete a line), so you can see exactly which moves you know and which you don't.

  • Each attempt at a move starts at 100%; finding the move on your first try keeps it at 100%. Each mistake before the right move costs a penalty, each time you make it. The defaults are below; you can change each one (0% to 100%) on the Options page, and your past attempts are re-scored straight away.
  • Alternative move, −20%: one of your study's unmarked alternative moves.
  • Masters move, −20%: a move among the top 5 master moves in that position that masters play 10% of the time or more (in Lichess's Masters database). You can change "top 5" to anything from top 1 to top 5 on the Options page. Other moves masters play count as errors. A move that is both an alternative and a masters move costs the alternative's penalty.
  • Error, −50%: an alternative your study marks ?!, ? or ??, or any other wrong move.
  • Hint, −100%: the Hint, and Show correct move. Move accuracy never goes below 0%.
  • Each move keeps its last 10 attempts, including everything you tried. Its move accuracy is their average, as a percentage, with recent attempts counting more: the latest counts 100%, the one before 90%, then 80%, down to 10% for the 10th. E.g. attempts of 0%, 80%, 100%, 100% (oldest to newest) give 74%. Move accuracies are refreshed each time you open the app, so new data (such as master games) is taken into account.
  • Not counted: a line you leave unfinished.
  • Highlighting: moves you know (100%) look plain. Moves that need work get a violet background, stronger the weaker they are: 90–99% faint, 70–89% medium, 69% or less strong. Moves you haven't tried yet, and the opponent's moves, look plain too. As on Lichess, a move's text is colored only when the study gives it a glyph (e.g. ! green, ? orange). (Violet is used so it isn't confused with the red and amber that chess sites use for bad moves; your highlighted moves are the right moves, ones you keep missing.)
What counts as a try
  • Playing a line from the start: one try for each of your moves in it.
  • Partway through: one try for each of your moves from the starting point on; the moves before it aren't counted.
  • Test single positions: one try of the move tested.
  • Leaving a line before it ends (for example when Practice starts afresh after you change the selection) records nothing.
When lines and positions were last practiced

The app also keeps track of when you practice, for spaced repetition (to be added later). Nothing shows these times yet.

  • Lines: a line counts as practiced only when you play it all the way through, from move 1 to the end. Its time is when you attempted its last move, and the last 10 times are kept. A line started Partway through, a line you stop with End line here, a line you leave unfinished, and Test single positions don't count for the line; they only count for the moves you played.
  • Positions: each position where you have to find a move keeps the time you last attempted it, in any mode, including Test single positions and the move you're on when you press End line here (if you'd already tried something). So a position can have been practiced more recently than any line through it.
  • Times are kept only from now on, are included in backups, and are dropped for lines and moves that are no longer in your studies after Update studies.
Which lines share a move accuracy

Each move accuracy belongs to one of your moves at one point in a chapter: the chapter plus the moves leading to it. Every line that passes through that point shares it, so getting 3. Bc4 right in one line counts for all the lines that continue from it. The same position in a different chapter, or reached by a different move order, keeps its own move accuracy.

Where you see your statistics
  • Studies page: each study and chapter shows the average move accuracy of the moves you've tried in it, e.g. "82%" (hover over it for an explanation), highlighted the same way. Nothing is shown until you've tried a move there.
  • End of a line: in the move list, the moves that lost points this time get a violet background; hover over one to see its points and the wrong moves you played.
  • A chapter's move tree (click the ▸ next to a chapter on the Studies page): every move of the chapter, with yours highlighted by move accuracy.
  • Small board: in a chapter's move tree, and in the practice move list once a line is over, hover over (or tap) any move. For your moves it shows the position you had to answer, with a green arrow for the correct move, and a red arrow for each wrong move you've played in the last 10 tries, and above it the move with how often it's played in Lichess games, e.g. "5. c3 (Lichess games 45%)" (once the statistics have downloaded), then e.g. "75% move accuracy (last 10 tries)" and "Wrong: Bc4 ×2, d3". For the opponent's moves it shows the position after the move, with the move as a white arrow, or a red arrow if your study marks it ?, ?? or ?!.
When statistics are dropped
  • After Update studies, move accuracies for moves that no longer exist in your studies are dropped; everything else is kept.
  • Statistics live only in this browser. Use a backup (Options page) to keep them safe or move them.

Weaknesses

  • The Weaknesses page lists your most frequent weak positions. Under the Position heading, choose how many to show (25, 50, 75 or 100); under Move accuracy, choose the highest move accuracy to include (≤ 90% down to ≤ 10%).
  • Each row is one of your moves. Its first line is the study and chapter (e.g. "My repertoire · Italian"); the second, in grey, names the position the way chess books do: the deepest opening name that applies, then the moves played since that named position, up to the position where it's your move (e.g. "Italian Game: Giuoco Piano, 4. c3 ♘f6 5. d4 exd4"; at most the last 3 moves are shown, after "…"). Then Position reached (times): how many times the position was reached in rated Lichess games; and your move accuracy there (over its last 10 tries). The most frequent positions come first.
  • Only moves you've tried are listed. A position in two chapters has a row for each, since each chapter keeps its own statistics. Positions whose Lichess statistics haven't downloaded yet are left out until they have.
  • Hover over a row to see the small board, with the correct move and the wrong moves you've played.

How lines are chosen

  • With Prefer lines I miss (and Main line only), lines with moves you often get wrong come up more often, and new lines are introduced fairly soon. The other Opponent's moves choices ignore your statistics.
  • The same line is never given twice in a row when there's another to choose.
How the weighting works

Each of your moves gets a weight of 1 + 4 × how far its move accuracy is below 100%: a move at 100% has weight 1, one at 0% has weight 5, and one you haven't tried yet has weight 3. A line's chance of coming up is its weakest move's weight, not the average, so one bad move is enough to bring a line back. For example, a line where every move is 100% has weight 1, but if one move is at 50% the line has weight 3, so it comes up three times as often. In Test single positions, the move tested within the line is picked by the same weights.

Options and backups

  • Options: mistake penalties and what counts as a masters move, updating your studies, the progress of the automatic Lichess downloads (move statistics and masters statistics), your Lichess token, and backups.
  • Backup: Save backup saves a file with your stats, settings and downloaded Lichess data (move statistics and masters statistics, so they needn't be downloaded again) (in Chrome and Edge you choose where; Safari and Firefox put it in your download folder, unless you set them to ask where to save downloads). Restore backup loads it again, on another browser or after clearing your browser. It never includes your token.

Mistake penalties

How much of a move's accuracy each mistake costs, each time you make it before finding the right move. A move found first time has 100% move accuracy; it never goes below 0%. Changing these recalculates the move accuracy of all your past attempts straight away.

Lichess studies

Update studies downloads all your Lichess studies again, private ones included, so changes you've made on Lichess (new chapters, moves, variations or comments) appear here. Your statistics are kept, except for moves that are no longer in your studies.

Move frequency

How often each move is played in all rated Lichess games, shown on the small boards when you hover over a move, and in the Masters database (over-the-board games between players rated 2200+ FIDE), saved for later use. The statistics for positions that don't have them yet download automatically in the background after Update studies (and resume when you open the app); you can keep practicing meanwhile.
