Getting started

  1. Make your repertoire on Lichess (lichess.org/study). One study per opening, one chapter per line. Set each chapter's Orientation to the side you play: that decides which moves you're tested on. Mark moves with ?, ?! and so on as you like.
  2. Connect to Lichess (Lichess page). Your studies are fetched. (Opened from disk, it opens Lichess's token page instead: create a token, copy it and paste it into the box.)
  3. Restore a backup, or download the statistics. Used the app before? Restore backup. Otherwise check the rating bands and time controls (Move statistics, suggested from your Lichess rating) and press Download statistics.
  4. Choose what to practise. Start with All, or make a group on the Studies page (Groups, ✎).
  5. Practise. On the Practice page, choose Lines (whole lines) or Positions (single moves) and press Start practice. Hint helps when you're stuck.
  • Look through a chapter first on the Studies page (offline too).
  • Save a backup now and then (Lichess page): your stats live only in this browser.
  • Install it as an app: on an iPad or iPhone, Safari's Share button → Add to Home Screen; in Chrome or Edge, the install icon in the address bar. The installed app keeps its own data: connect to Lichess there, and restore a backup to bring your stats.

Statistics download in the background; you can practise meanwhile. New studies appear the next time the app opens. Once opened online, the app works offline, except for anything that needs Lichess.

Words used in the app
  • Line: one path through a chapter: one of your study's moves at each of your turns, and every reply your study gives the opponent.
  • Alternative move: another move for you in your study, e.g. 3. Bb5 where your main move is 3. Bc4. It's correct too, unless the study marks it ?, ?? or ?!, or Main lines only is ticked.
  • Move accuracy: how often you were right first time in a position, over your last 10 tries (see Practice).
  • Line accuracy: the share of your moves right first time in a completed line.
  • Error: any move you play that isn't one of your study's moves, or the hint. Errors cost move accuracy.
  • Mistake: a move a study marks ? (mistake), ?? (blunder) or ?! (dubious). Not the same as an error.
  • Masters move: one of the top 3 moves in master games (over the board, players rated 2200+ FIDE) in that position, played 10% of the time or more.
  • Position reached: how many times a position was reached in rated Lichess games, and in master games, e.g. "Reached 23k times (Lichess) 46 times (Masters)".
Where your data is kept

Everything (your token, studies, stats and settings) stays in this browser. The app only talks to Lichess, to fetch your studies and move statistics. To keep your stats safe or move them to another browser, save a backup (see the Lichess page).

Practice

  • Choose Lines (whole lines) or Positions (one of your moves at a time, from move 3), from the chapters of the chosen group (Studies to practice). Main lines only keeps to the main moves, for both sides: only your main move is correct.
  • Spaced repetition chooses what comes next; the sliders in the Spaced repetition tile under Studies to practice (▾ opens it) change the balance, and Show lines and Show positions there list what's most likely to come up next.
  • Press Start practice. Practice carries on until End practice.
  • Play your moves on the board. Any of your study's moves is correct (not one it marks ?, ?? or ?!), and the line follows the one you play; with Main lines only, just the main move. Keep trying as long as you like. Each error gets a short message.
  • A line behind one of your other choices (e.g. your 3. Bb5 where the main move is 3. Bc4) starts just after it, set up on the board, so it gets practised too.
  • A chapter set to both sides in its group (see Studies) gives lines for each side; the heading says e.g. "(as black)" for its other side.
  • Hint circles the piece to move; pressed again (Show correct move), it draws the move. Both count as an error.
  • Skip goes on to the next one; End practice stops. The moves you've answered still count.
  • Comments from your study appear as blue bubbles in the move list; hover over one to read it. Click a move, or use ◀ ▶ or the arrow keys, to look back.
  • At the end of a line you see how it went, e.g. "5 of 6 moves correct" and "83% line accuracy". Press Next line (Next position for positions), or Enter.
  • You can visit other pages mid-line; Practice takes you back. The bell under the board turns sounds on and off.
Error messages

One fact per line, e.g.:

  • What the move is: "Not your preferred move", "Alternative move, not your preferred move" (with Main lines only), or "Alternative move marked as a mistake" with your study's answer, e.g. "Your study answers 4… ♘xe4".
  • How masters rate it: "#3 masters move (12%)" or "Not a masters move".
  • How often it's played on Lichess: "#4 Lichess move (8%)" or "Not played in Lichess games".

What the move is shows in violet, as errors do on the boards; the masters line in teal for a masters move, the Lichess line in grey for a top-3 Lichess move. After 3 seconds the message gives way to "Your move (3 of 5 in line)". After an error, Hint also shows what your study says about the move you tried.

The small board

Hover over (or tap) a move in the move list, the move tree or the Show lines table.

  • Your move: the position you had to answer, with arrows for your study's moves and each error (see Arrow colours). Then the position's move accuracy, when you last tested it, and your errors in the same colors.
  • The opponent's move: the position after it, with its arrow.
  • Above both: the move's rank and share among Lichess and master games, e.g. "#2 Lichess move (27%)", and how often the position is reached, e.g. "Reached 23k times (Lichess) 46 times (Masters)".

Move accuracy

Each try in a position is right (100%) if your first move is one of your study's moves, otherwise wrong (0%): any other move, a move the study marks ?, ?? or ?!, or the hint. Move accuracy is the share right first time over your last 10 tries. Below 100%, a move gets a violet background.

Each position has two: with Main lines only, only the main move is right (tries without it where you played one of your alternatives are left out); without it, any of your study's moves. You see the one for the current setting.

What counts as a try
  • Each of your moves in a line, and the move in a single position.
  • With Skip or End practice: the moves answered so far, and the move you're on if you'd already tried something there.
  • Every line through the same position in a chapter shares its accuracy, whichever of your moves you play there. The same position in another chapter, or by another move order, has its own.
  • When your studies are updated, accuracies of positions no longer in them are dropped.
Spaced repetition

Before each line or position, every one gets a priority, and the next is picked at random in proportion to it. By default, common, weak, overdue and untried ones come up more. The same one never comes twice in a row when there's another.

  • The four sliders, in the Spaced repetition tile on the Practice page, set how much each counts: Frequency (how often opponents on Lichess lead to it), Accuracy (weak ones), Last played (overdue ones) and Untried. 0% ignores it, 50% is normal, 100% makes it dominate. Untried at 0% leaves untried ones out; at 100% they come first. Reset puts them back to 50%.
  • Show lines (or Show positions) lists the 25 most likely to be tested next, with their position reached, accuracy, when last tested and chance of coming up next.
How the priority is worked out

With every slider at 50%: priority = frequency × need. Each slider raises its part to a power: 0 at 0%, 1 at 50%, 6 at 100%.

  • Frequency: 0.2 to 1, by the shares of the opponent's moves on the way, multiplied (a line: all of them; your own moves don't count, so a rare choice of yours doesn't sink its lines), on a log scale; 0.6 until the statistics download.
  • Need: 6 if never tried; otherwise weakness × due.
  • Weakness: 1 + 2 × (1 − accuracy): 1 at 100%, 3 at 0%.
  • Due: days since last tested ÷ the review interval, between 0.1 and 3 (3 if unknown). The interval is 27 × accuracy days: 1 day at 0%, about 11 at 50%, about 30 at 70%, 128 at 100%.
  • Last tested: a line counts only when played all the way through, from its start (not when skipped or ended early); a position whenever it's tried, in a line or on its own, so it can be more recent than any line through it. A line uses its line accuracy (untried positions count as 0%); a position, its move accuracy.

Studies

  • The Studies page shows a chapter on a board (offline too), with your studies and their chapters beside it: click a study's name or ▸ to show its chapters, and a chapter to open it. Hover over a chapter's name to see its opening; the dot after it shows the side you play. The header's Studies button goes back to the chapter and position you last looked at.
  • Under the board, choose the arrows (see Arrow colours): Study (your study's moves and its own arrows and circles), Masters (the masters moves), Lichess (the top 5 Lichess moves), each with how often it's played, or Errors (your errors in this position in the last 10 tries, "×2" when made twice, and your study's moves, the main one with the move accuracy). Hover over the square an arrow points to for more. Beside the board: the moves (with how often the position is reached) and the move's annotation (e.g. !) and comment. Your move accuracy shows on the small board when you hover over one of your moves. ←/→ step through the moves, ↑/↓ change chapter.
  • During practice, the small board icon after the study's name ends practice and opens the chapter here, at the position on the board.
  • Groups: what you practise is always the group chosen on the Practice page (Studies to practice): All (every chapter, from its own side; it can't be renamed or deleted) or one of your own. Only groups decide what you practise.
  • ✎ in the Studies page's Groups tile opens Edit group, with every study and chapter: choose a group, then click a chapter's circle (or its name) to choose: not played (a dashed ring), its own side (white or black), or both sides (half and half). Then Save, Create new (a new group from the chapters chosen; untick All first to start empty), Rename, Delete or Cancel (or Esc, or ×). For All, only Create new and Cancel.
  • Chapters no longer on Lichess are left out of every group. Groups are included in backups.
Hiding studies and chapters

The eye beside the Studies heading shows hidden studies and chapters (in grey), each with its own eye: click it to hide or unhide that study or chapter. Click the eye beside Studies again to put them out of sight. Hiding only tidies the list: it doesn't change what's practised.

The move tree
  • The main line runs along the top; + after a move opens its variations, − closes them.
  • Your moves with move accuracy below 100% have a violet background (moves of the other side too, once you've played it). Your alternatives marked ?, ?? or ?!, and the moves after them, are in italics (they aren't tested).
  • Moves your study marks with a glyph take its color, as on Lichess (! green, ? orange).
  • Hover over a move to see it on a small board; click it to show it on the big board.

Lichess

  • The header's Lichess button shows a red dot when you're not connected (including when Lichess has ended the connection). Other messages, such as Lichess asking to slow down, show only on the Lichess page.
  • The Lichess page's cards: Status (your account and Lichess rating, when you connected, when your studies were last fetched, and any message about fetching your studies), Connection (Connect to Lichess or Disconnect), Move statistics, Backup and, after your first connection, Getting started (Download statistics). Before you connect, only Status and Connection show.
  • Move statistics: how many positions have their move statistics and masters statistics so far, and the Lichess games the move statistics come from: rating bands (a game's band is its players' average Lichess rating, which runs higher than Chess.com's; e.g. Chess.com 1500 rapid is about Lichess 1750–1800) and time controls, all combined, so the busiest count most. To change them, unlock the padlock, tick, and press Apply: every position's statistics then download again in the background (the old ones are used meanwhile). They're first set from your Lichess rating: its band and the next one up (1200–2000 without a rating), in blitz and rapid; after that they only change when you change them.
  • Backup: Save backup saves your stats, settings and Lichess statistics in a file; Restore backup loads one, e.g. in another browser. Backups never include your token.
  • Disconnect ends the connection (also on Lichess); your studies and stats stay, but stop updating until you Connect to Lichess again. Lichess also ends a connection after about a year: connect again then. Opened from disk, it's Add token (Lichess opens to create one, to paste into the box) and Clear token.
  • Your studies are updated from Lichess each time the app opens, so changes you make there appear here; reload the page to update them now. If you're practicing at the time, the update waits until you press End practice. Your stats are kept, except for moves no longer in your studies.
Backups
  • Chrome and Edge ask where to save. Safari and Firefox use your download folder (in Safari, Settings → General → File download location → Ask for each download lets you choose).
  • Restoring replaces your stats and settings, and merges the Lichess statistics with those already here (the newer copy of each wins). Backups from earlier versions of the app can't be restored.
Move statistics

How often each move is played in rated Lichess games and in master games. They download in the background whenever the app opens (after your studies are updated), and carry on later by themselves if Lichess can't be reached. Positions no game reaches are skipped. When they're complete, the Lichess page's Backup card suggests a backup, so they needn't be downloaded again.
