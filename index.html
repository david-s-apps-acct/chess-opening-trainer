Welcome to Chess Opening Trainer

Practice the openings in your Lichess studies. The app quizzes you on your moves, remembers which ones you know, and brings back the ones you don't.

Sign in to Lichess if asked, then create the token (the access needed is already chosen), copy it, and paste it here.

Your studies stay in your browser; the app only talks to Lichess.

Getting started

  1. Connect to Lichess: sign in to Lichess if asked and allow the app to read your studies; you're brought back and your studies, private ones included, are fetched. (Opened from disk, it opens Lichess's token page instead: create a token, copy it and paste it into the box.)
  2. Used the app before (in another browser, say)? Restore backup brings back your stats and the move statistics; otherwise press Start.
  3. Studies: tick the chapters you want to practice.
  4. Practice: choose Lines or Positions and press Start practice.

Move statistics from Lichess and master games download in the background; you can practice meanwhile.

No Lichess account? Try sample studies (online only) loads a Ruy Lopez and a Queen's Gambit Declined study with their statistics. Add a token later (Options) and your own studies replace them.

Words used in the app
  • Line: one path through a chapter: your main move at each of your turns, and every reply your study gives the opponent.
  • Alternative move: another move for you in your study, e.g. 3. Bb5 where your main move is 3. Bc4. Alternatives aren't tested; only the main move is correct.
  • Move accuracy: how well you know one of your moves, from 0% to 100% (see Practice).
  • Line accuracy: the average move accuracy of your moves in a completed line.
  • Error: any move you play that isn't your study's move, or the hint. Errors cost move accuracy.
  • Mistake: a move a study marks ? (mistake), ?? (blunder) or ?! (dubious). Not the same as an error.
  • Masters move: one of the top 5 moves in master games (over the board, players rated 2200+ FIDE) in that position, played 10% of the time or more. "Top 5" can be changed on the Options page.
  • Position reached: how many times a position was reached in rated Lichess games, e.g. "Position reached 23k times".
Where your data is kept

Everything (your token, studies, stats and settings) stays in this browser. The app only talks to Lichess, to fetch your studies and move statistics. To keep your stats safe or move them to another browser, save a backup (see Options).

Studies

  • Tick studies or chapters, or All, to choose what you practice. The count at the top right shows how many chapters are ticked.
  • Click a study's name, ▸ or "7 chapters" to show its chapters. Click a chapter's name or ▸ to show its moves; click "3 lines" to show all its variations too. Clicking a name doesn't tick it.
  • Hover over a chapter's name to see its opening. The dot after it shows the side you play (the orientation set on Lichess).
  • Each study and chapter shows your average move accuracy once you've tried a move there, with a violet background below 100%.
Presets
  • A preset is a saved set of ticked chapters. Choose one from Presets to tick exactly its chapters.
  • The pencil (✎) opens the buttons: Save as new saves the ticked chapters under a new name; Update saves them in the chosen preset; Rename and Delete (the ticks stay).
  • When the ticks differ from the chosen preset, its name is greyed out; choose it again to go back to its chapters.
  • Chapters no longer on Lichess, or in hidden studies, are skipped. Presets are included in backups.
Hiding studies

The eye at the top shows hidden studies (in grey), each with its own eye: click it to hide or unhide that study. Click the top eye again to put them out of sight. Hidden studies aren't practiced.

The move tree
  • The main line runs along the top; + after a move opens its variations, − closes them.
  • Your moves with move accuracy below 100% have a violet background. Your alternative moves, and the moves after them, are in italics (they aren't tested).
  • Moves your study marks with a glyph take its color, as on Lichess (! green, ? orange).
  • Hover over a move to see the small board (see Practice).

Practice

  • Choose Lines (whole lines, from move 1) or Positions (one of your moves at a time, from move 3), from the chapters ticked on the Studies page. Main lines only leaves out the opponent's side variations.
  • Spaced repetition chooses what comes next; the sliders under Spaced repetition focus change the balance, and Show lines lists what's most likely next.
  • Press Start practice. Practice carries on until End practice.
  • Play your moves on the board. Only your study's main move is correct; keep trying as long as you like. Each error gets a short message.
  • Hint circles the piece to move; pressed again (Show correct move), it draws the move. Both count as an error.
  • Skip goes on to the next one; End practice stops. The moves you've answered still count.
  • Comments from your study appear as blue bubbles in the move list; hover over one to read it. Click a move, or use ◀ ▶ or the arrow keys, to look back.
  • At the end of a line you see how it went, e.g. "5 of 6 moves correct" and "83% line accuracy". Press Next line (Next position for positions), or Enter.
  • You can visit other pages mid-line; Practice takes you back. The bell under the board turns sounds on and off.
Error messages

One fact per line, e.g.:

  • What the move is: "Not your preferred move", "Alternative move, not your preferred move", or "Alternative move marked as a mistake" with your study's answer, e.g. "Your study answers 4… ♘xe4".
  • How masters rate it: "#3 masters move (12%)" or "Not a masters move".
  • How often it's played on Lichess: "#4 Lichess move (8%)" or "Not played in Lichess games".

Red means bad news; blue marks an alternative or masters move, amber a move your study marks ?!, ? or ??. After 3 seconds the message gives way to "Your move (3 of 5 in line)". After an error, Hint also shows what your study says about the move you tried.

The small board

Hover over (or tap) a move in the move list, the move tree or the Show lines table.

  • Your move: the position you had to answer, a green arrow for the correct move and a pale arrow for each error (blue: alternative or masters move; amber: an alternative marked ?!, ? or ??; red: any other error). Then its move accuracy, when you last tested it, and your errors in the same colors.
  • The opponent's move: the position after it, with a white arrow (amber if your study marks it ?!, ? or ??).
  • Above both: the move's rank and share among Lichess and master games, e.g. "#2 Lichess move (27%)", and "Position reached 23k times".
Move accuracy

Each try at one of your moves starts at 100%. Each error before the right move costs a penalty, every time you make it (set on the Options page; never below 0%):

ErrorDefault
Alternative move (unmarked)−20%
Masters move−20%
Any other move, or an alternative marked ?!, ? or ??−50%
Hint, or Show correct move−100%

A move's accuracy is the average of its last 10 tries, the latest counting most (100%, then 90%, 80% … down to 10%). Moves below 100% get a violet background.

What counts as a try
  • Each of your moves in a line, and the move in a single position.
  • With Skip or End practice: the moves answered so far, and the move you're on if you'd already tried something there.
  • Every line through the same point in a chapter shares that move's accuracy. The same position in another chapter, or by another move order, has its own.
  • After Update studies, accuracies of moves no longer in your studies are dropped.
Spaced repetition

Before each line or position, every one gets a priority, and the next is picked at random in proportion to it. By default, common, weak, overdue and untried ones come up more. The same one never comes twice in a row when there's another.

  • The four sliders set how much each counts: Frequency (how often the position is reached on Lichess), Accuracy (weak ones), Last played (overdue ones) and Untried. 0% ignores it, 50% is normal, 100% makes it dominate. Untried at 0% leaves untried ones out; at 100% they come first. Reset puts them back to 50%.
  • Show lines (or Show positions) lists the 25 most likely next, with their position reached, accuracy, when last tested and chance of coming up next.
How the priority is worked out

With every slider at 50%: priority = frequency × need. Each slider raises its part to a power: 0 at 0%, 1 at 50%, 6 at 100%.

  • Frequency: 0.2 to 1, by the times the position was reached on Lichess (a line: its final position), on a log scale; 0.6 until its statistics download.
  • Need: 6 if never tried; otherwise weakness × due.
  • Weakness: 1 + 2 × (1 − accuracy): 1 at 100%, 3 at 0%.
  • Due: days since last tested ÷ the review interval, between 0.1 and 3 (3 if unknown). The interval is 27 × accuracy days: 1 day at 0%, about 11 at 50%, about 30 at 70%, 128 at 100%.
  • Last tested: a line counts only when played all the way through, from move 1 (not when skipped or ended early); a position whenever its move is tried, in a line or on its own, so it can be more recent than any line through it. A line uses its line accuracy (untried moves count as 0%); a position, its move accuracy.

Reverse sides

Reverse sides (not scored), for Lines: you play the opponent's moves from your studies and the app plays yours. Any reply your study gives is correct, and the line follows it; with Main lines only, just the main reply. Hint gives the main reply. Nothing is recorded, and lines come up by how often they're reached (the sliders aren't used).

Options

Hover over a heading or a penalty on the Options page to see what it's for.

  • Error penalties: the move accuracy each kind of error costs (see Practice, Move accuracy), and how many top master moves count as masters moves. Changes apply to your past tries straight away; Reset puts the penalties and Masters moves back to their defaults.
  • Lichess studies: Update studies fetches your studies again after you change them on Lichess; your stats are kept. Below it: your account, when your studies were fetched, and how many positions have move statistics so far.
  • Lichess token: Add token opens Lichess to create one; paste it into the box and it's saved. Clear token removes it from this browser (your studies stay).
  • Backup: Save backup saves your stats, settings and Lichess statistics in a file; Restore backup loads one, e.g. in another browser. Backups never include your token.
Move statistics

How often each move is played in rated Lichess games and in master games. They download in the background after Update studies and whenever the app opens, and carry on later by themselves if Lichess can't be reached. Positions no game reaches are skipped. When they're complete, the app suggests a backup, so they needn't be downloaded again.

Backups
  • Chrome and Edge ask where to save. Safari and Firefox use your download folder (in Safari, Settings → General → File download location → Ask for each download lets you choose).
  • Restoring replaces your stats and settings, and merges the Lichess statistics with those already here (the newer copy of each wins). Backups from earlier versions of the app can't be restored.
